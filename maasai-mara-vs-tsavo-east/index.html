Tsavo East – The Land of Red Elephants

Tsavo East is Kenya's largest national park – a sprawling 13,747 km² of untamed savannah, dotted with baobabs and bisected by the Galana River. It's famous for its iconic "red elephants", which bathe in the park's volcanic soil, giving their skin a striking reddish‑brown hue. This is raw, authentic Africa – fewer tourists, vast horizons, and wildlife that thrives year‑round because of permanent water sources like the Aruba Dam.

Beyond elephants, you'll encounter lions (including the legendary maneless males of Tsavo), leopards, cheetahs, buffalo, giraffes, zebras, hippos, crocodiles, and over 500 bird species. The park's history is equally gripping – it's where the infamous "Man‑eaters of Tsavo" terrorised railway workers in 1898.

For Watamu guests, Tsavo East is the perfect safari add‑on – just 2.5 hours by private transfer. You can be watching elephants at sunset by late afternoon on your first day.

🔥 Marafa – Hell's Kitchen

Just 20 minutes north of Watamu lies Marafa, a geological wonder often called "Hell's Kitchen". This deep sandstone canyon was carved by millions of years of erosion, creating a labyrinth of gorges, ridges, and natural sculptures that glow orange, red, and gold in the sunset light.

Walk through the canyon's narrow passageways, discover hidden crevices, and learn about the local legends that surround this mystical place. The best time to visit is late afternoon – as the sun lowers, the canyon walls ignite in a spectacular display of colour.

  • 🏜️ Age: Over 20 million years old
  • 🎨 Colours: Deep reds, burnt oranges, golden yellows
  • 🌅 Best time: Late afternoon – the sunset is unforgettable
  • 📸 Perfect for: Photography, hiking, and cultural storytelling

Marafa's sandstone canyon – a photographer's dream at golden hour.
